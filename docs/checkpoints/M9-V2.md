## 1. Executive Summary

Milestone **M9-V2** evaluates the frozen M8B decentralized coordination architecture under intense spatial congestion, narrow aisles, dense fleet deployment, and concentrated task traffic. Unlike M9-V1 (which validated topological expansion on a 32m grid with broad corridors and 5 AMRs), M9-V2 deliberately stresses the coordination mechanisms:

- **24 Industrial Storage Racks** in a $6 \times 4$ array.
- **Narrow Aisles ($1.8\,\text{m}$ width)** restricting passing maneuverability.
- **Central Delivery Hub** with 4 clustered dropoff bays inducing severe traffic convergence.
- **10 Autonomous Mobile Robots** operating concurrently in Gazebo Harmonic.
- **30 Concentrated Tasks** originating at 12 perimeter stations and terminating at the 4 central bays.

In strict adherence to experimental protocols, **no coordination algorithms, reservation heuristics, PIBT parameters, or safety controller thresholds were altered**. The pilot trial executed under full ROS 2 instrumentation and terminated via an authentic, non-fatal `SAFETY_ABORT` at $T = 139.96\,\text{s}$ due to a $1\,\text{mm}$ proximity-threshold breach ($0.349\,\text{m} < 0.350\,\text{m}$) in a shared cross-aisle.

The raw telemetry has been permanently preserved as the canonical failure trial (`exp_m9_w30_t0_s42_adaptive_1789373423.json`). This report presents the comprehensive design-level failure analysis and evaluates remediation options across five formal classes.

## 4. Failure Analysis and Remediation Options

### 4.1 Itemized Technical Investigation

#### 1. Exact Continuous Robot Geometry at the Breach
- **Timestamp**: $T = 1789373575.369043$ ($139.96\,\text{s}$ elapsed mission time).
- **Position of `amr_1`**: $(x_1, y_1) = (9.888, 30.250)\,\text{m}$.
- **Position of `amr_4`**: $(x_2, y_2) = (10.237, 30.250)\,\text{m}$.
- **Separation Vector**: $\Delta x = 10.237 - 9.888 = 0.349\,\text{m}$, $\Delta y = 0.000\,\text{m}$.
- **Euclidean Center-to-Center Distance**: $d = \sqrt{0.349^2 + 0.000^2} = \mathbf{0.349\,\text{m}}$.
- **Context**: The breach occurred in the **North Cross-Aisle** ($y \approx 30.25\,\text{m}$), directly north of `rack_21` (top of rack at $y = 29.0\,\text{m}$, north perimeter wall at $y = 31.8\,\text{m}$). Both AMRs were oriented longitudinally along the aisle (heading East toward the central hub), traveling in single file.

#### 2. Robot Footprint Dimensions versus the 0.35 m Safety Criterion
- **Physical Vehicle Footprint** (from `urdf/amr_core.xacro` and `config/robots/amr_default.yaml`):
  - Chassis Length: $L = 0.65\,\text{m}$.
  - Chassis Width: $W = 0.45\,\text{m}$ (outer wheel-to-wheel span: $0.49\,\text{m}$).
  - Circumscribed Radius: $R_{\text{circumscribed}} = \sqrt{(0.65/2)^2 + (0.49/2)^2} = \sqrt{0.325^2 + 0.245^2} \approx 0.407\,\text{m}$.
  - Circumscribed Diameter: $2R \approx 0.814\,\text{m}$.
- **Safety Criterion**:
  - The benchmark harness evaluates the scalar center-to-center distance: $d = \|\mathbf{p}_1 - \mathbf{p}_2\|_2 \ge 0.350\,\text{m}$.
- **Geometric Incompatibility**:
  - If two identical rectangular robots of length $0.65\,\text{m}$ are collinear in head-to-tail formation ($\Delta y = 0$, yaw $= 0$), their bumpers touch when their center-to-center distance is exactly $L = 0.650\,\text{m}$.
  - At a center-to-center separation of $d = 0.350\,\text{m}$, two collinear robots would experience an overlapping bounding volume of $0.65 - 0.35 = 0.30\,\text{m}$ ($30\,\text{cm}$) if perfectly aligned.
  - The $0.35\,\text{m}$ threshold in `amr_default.yaml` was originally designated as `collision_radius: 0.35` (the radius of *one* robot). Two non-overlapping circular envelopes of radius $0.35\,\text{m}$ require center-to-center clearance $d \ge 0.70\,\text{m}$. Evaluating center-to-center distance directly against $0.35\,\text{m}$ is a legacy instrumentation inconsistency.

#### 3. Geometric Clearance of the 0.5 m Planning Grid
- Discrete planning resolution: $\Delta = 0.5\,\text{m}$.
- Adjacent cells in 4-connected grid space have center-to-center distance: $\Delta = 0.500\,\text{m}$.
- At the breach:
  - `amr_1` was in cell $(19, 60)$ (cell range $X \in [9.5, 10.0]$, center $9.75\,\text{m}$).
  - `amr_4` was in cell $(20, 60)$ (cell range $X \in [10.0, 10.5]$, center $10.25\,\text{m}$).
- In discrete space-time reservations (RHCR/PIBT), cells $(19, 60)$ and $(20, 60)$ are **distinct and validly non-conflicting**.
- However, because the physical vehicle length ($0.65\,\text{m}$) exceeds the discrete cell size ($0.50\,\text{m}$), **a single-cell reservation does NOT provide adequate geometric clearance for longitudinal queuing**. Two vehicles occupying adjacent cells along their heading vector have nominal bumper clearance of $0.50 - 0.65 = -0.15\,\text{m}$ (negative clearance).

#### 4. Physical Compatibility of M9-V2 Aisle Width
- **North-South Aisles**: Width is $1.8\,\text{m}$ ($X \in [3.6, 5.4]$).
  - Physical clearance: Two AMRs side-by-side require $2 \times 0.49 = 0.98\,\text{m}$. In theory, an open $1.8\,\text{m}$ aisle leaves $0.82\,\text{m}$ total lateral clearance ($0.27\,\text{m}$ per side and between).
  - Grid clearance: At $0.5\,\text{m}$ resolution, $1.8\,\text{m}$ accommodates 3 discrete cells. If two AMRs navigate in opposite directions, each occupies an outer cell, leaving only the center cell for transition. Any yaw angle or turning radius sweeps a footprint diameter of $0.814\,\text{m}$, fully closing the aisle.
- **North Cross-Aisle**: Width is $2.8\,\text{m}$ ($Y \in [29.0, 31.8]$).
  - However, the perimeter stations (`pickup_n1` at $(4.5, 30.5)$, `pickup_n2` at $(7.5, 30.5)$) and charging pads ($Y=31.2$) project into this space, reducing the active travel corridor to $Y \in [29.2, 30.7]$ ($\approx 1.5\,\text{m}$ effective width).
  - The bidirectional traffic model allows robots to choose paths independently without directional lane conventions, forcing tandem queuing in shared narrow corridors.

#### 5. Breach Attribution: Primary Causes & Interactions
The breach was caused by an **interaction between three distinct factors**:
1. **Discrete Grid vs Footprint Discrepancy**: The discrete MAPF planner treats adjacent $0.5\,\text{m}$ cells as collision-free, despite vehicle length ($0.65\,\text{m}$) requiring $\ge 2$ cells separation along the longitudinal axis.
2. **Task Convergence & Shared Corridor Geometry**: `task_25` (`amr_4` leaving pickup $(4.5, 30.5)$) and `task_22` (`amr_1` leaving pickup $(7.5, 30.5)$) both required eastbound transit along the same narrow North Cross-Aisle ($y \approx 30.25\,\text{m}$) to reach the central delivery hub.
3. **Continuous Tracking Dynamics**: `amr_4` slowed/paused at $x = 10.237$ ($-0.013\,\text{m}$ behind cell center), while `amr_1` tracked forward in cell 19 to $x = 9.888$ ($+0.138\,\text{m}$ past cell center). Continuous deceleration and tracking errors compressed their center-to-center separation from the nominal $0.500\,\text{m}$ to $0.349\,\text{m}$.

#### 6. Analysis of the 8,437 Safety Interventions
- **Instrumentation Mechanism**: In `scripts/run_benchmark.py`, `_make_scan_cb()` appends to `safety_brake_events` whenever `min(ranges) <= 0.40m` in a full $360^\circ$ LiDAR scan.
- **Rate and Duration**: At $10\,\text{Hz}$ across 10 robots over $140\,\text{s}$, the maximum possible scan count is $14,000$. The recorded 8,437 interventions represent **$60.3\%$ of all LiDAR scans having an object within $40\,\text{cm}$**.
- **Pathology vs Congestion Assessment**:
  - In `warehouse_m9_v2`, when robots dock at perimeter pickup stations $(y=1.5$ or $y=30.5)$ or charging pads, static obstacles (racks/walls) are within $0.40\,\text{m}$ of the turret. A stationary robot parked at a station accumulates **10 "interventions" per second**.
  - Total fleet replans remained modest (490 total across 10 robots, or $\approx 0.35\,\text{Hz}$ per robot), confirming that the motion controllers were NOT exhibiting high-frequency chattering or limit-cycle thrashing.
  - **Conclusion**: The 8,437 count is primarily an **instrumentation artifact of static obstacle proximity during station docking and queuing in narrow aisles**, combined with sustained spatial density, rather than a pathological control oscillation.

#### 7. Verification of Contact vs. Proximity Breach
- **Verification**: The recorded event is **strictly a proximity-threshold breach**, NOT physical bumper contact.
- In `run_benchmark.py`:
  ```python
  p1 = self.positions[r_ids[i]]
  p2 = self.positions[r_ids[j]]
  d = math.hypot(p1[0] - p2[0], p1[1] - p2[1])
  if d < 0.35:
      self.collision_events.append(...)
  ```
- The benchmark harness does not subscribe to Gazebo physics contact sensors; it computes center-to-center distance from continuous odometry. The event was triggered solely because $d = 0.349000\,\text{m} < 0.350000\,\text{m}$ (a margin breach of $1.0\,\text{mm}$).

### 6.1 Benchmark Instrumentation Separation (Class B)
The benchmark harness (`scripts/run_benchmark.py`) and core metrics infrastructure (`src/amr_fleet_core/amr_fleet_core/benchmark_manager.py`) were upgraded to report safety metrics as discrete, rigorously defined phenomena rather than conflating them:

1. **`minimum_center_to_center_distance_m`**:
   Euclidean distance between robot odometric centers $(x_i, y_i)$ and $(x_j, y_j)$ across all robot pairs throughout the trial. (Alias `minimum_inter_robot_distance_m` retained for full backwards compatibility).
2. **`proximity_breaches`**:
   Discrete count and detailed event list (`proximity_breach_events`) tracking occurrences where inter-robot center-to-center distance dipped below the $0.350\,\text{m}$ threshold ($d < 0.350\,\text{m}$).
3. **OBB Chassis-Overlap Samples / Geometric Contact Proxy (`physical_gazebo_contacts`)**:
   Geometric contact proxy computed using the 2D **Separating Axis Theorem (SAT)** for oriented bounding boxes (OBBs) representing the rectangular AMR chassis ($0.65\,\text{m} \times 0.45\,\text{m}$) using live vehicle poses $(x, y, \text{yaw})$. *Scientific Terminology Note*: Unless physical Gazebo contact sensor plugins are integrated directly onto bumper links, this metric strictly represents an **OBB chassis-overlap sample / geometric contact proxy** rather than physical contact sensor ground truth.
4. **`safety_brake_interventions`**:
   Edge-triggered count and event list (`safety_brake_events`) recording state transitions where an AMR's safety controller applies emergency braking due to an immediate obstacle hazard in its forward cone ($\pm 15^\circ$ arc at range $< 0.28\,\text{m}$). Eliminates false counting of static side obstacles (e.g. shelves during docking).
5. **`safety_aborts`**:
   Explicit binary metric ($1$ or $0$) recording whether the mission was terminated prematurely by safety abort policy.

### 6.2 Physical Geometry Correction (Class A)
To remediate the pathological North Cross-Aisle queue compression identified in Section 4.1:
- **Row 3 Racks (`rack_13` to `rack_18`)**: Shifted south from $y = 23.0\,\text{m}$ to $y = 22.0\,\text{m}$.
- **Row 4 Racks (`rack_19` to `rack_24`)**: Shifted south from $y = 27.5\,\text{m}$ to $y = 26.5\,\text{m}$.
- **North Cross-Aisle Widened**: The corridor width between Row 4 rack top ($y = 28.0\,\text{m}$) and the north wall ($y = 31.8\,\text{m}$) is expanded from $2.8\,\text{m}$ to **$3.8\,\text{m}$**.

## 8. M9-V2 Central Hub 4-Pillar Defense-in-Depth Remediation

Following the pre-remediation failure analysis approved by human review, the Central Delivery Hub failure was addressed via a 4-pillar defense-in-depth architecture:

1. **Pillar A: Waypoint / Task-Goal Consistency (`rh_planner.py`)**:
   - `replan()` explicitly overrides terminal waypoints to exact continuous station coordinates `(current_goal[0], current_goal[1])`.
   - Handles single-cell intra-cell transit when a robot is inside the station grid cell but $> 0.25\,\text{m}$ from the goal coordinate.
   - Triggers replanning if waypoints are exhausted without triggering goal arrival.

2. **Pillar B: Physical Footprint / Headway-Aware Reservation (`reservation_table.py`, `rh_node.py`)**:
   - Added `is_headway_conflict()` and `get_headway_conflict()` to `SpaceTimeReservationTable`.
   - Enforces a minimum discrete headway in the implemented reservation model ($k \ge 2$ discrete cells along the travel direction).
   - Addresses the observed adjacent-cell queueing failure mode and provides nominal physical clearance under the stated geometry ($\ge 1.000\,\text{m}$ discrete center separation, $+0.350\,\text{m}$ nominal physical clearance for $0.65\,\text{m}$ chassis length).
   - *Scoped Modeling Note*: The two-cell headway mechanism is a scoped mitigation for the identified collinear queueing failure mode under the implemented discrete reservation model and stated assumptions. It does not constitute a universal collision-free guarantee under arbitrary unmodeled dynamics, tracking perturbations, or out-of-lane interactions.
   - Integrated into `_evaluate_local_coordination()` and `_find_best_alternative_cell()`.

3. **Pillar C: Deterministic Local Safety Backstop (`CoordinationStatus.msg`, `rh_node.py`)**:
   - Augmented `CoordinationStatus.msg` with `float64 pose_x` and `float64 pose_y` transmitted over `/fleet/coordination_status` at $10\,\text{Hz}$.
   - Evaluates local forward corridor hazard ($0 < d_\parallel \le 0.700\,\text{m}$, $|d_\perp| \le 0.400\,\text{m}$) and commands $v = 0$ braking, bridging the LiDAR sub-$0.15\,\text{m}$ blind zone.

4. **Pillar D: Central Delivery Bay Shared-Resource Management (`rh_node.py`)**:
   - Capacity constraint ($\text{capacity} = 1$) on Bay 1 $(14.5, 14.5)$ (grid `(29, 29)`).
   - Approaching AMRs yield and hold at staging cell $(29, 33)$ ($2.0\,\text{m}$ discrete distance, $1.35\,\text{m}$ nominal clearance) until Bay 1 is clear.

5. **Regression & Component Validation Suite**:
   - 184 tests executed via `colcon test` across `amr_fleet_core`, `amr_fleet_sim`, and `amr_fleet_bringup`: **184 PASSED, 0 FAILURES, 0 ERRORS**.

It is **NOT Gazebo contact-sensor ground truth**, as physical bumper link contact plugins were not integrated.
